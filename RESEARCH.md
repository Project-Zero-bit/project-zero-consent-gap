# PROJECT ZERO — Expedition 003: Forceful entry / evidence-led wedge
Date: 12 October 2026

## Decision
**Chosen for prototype: trade-effluent records access and evidence workflow.** This is the quickest testable wedge for an unknown entrant *among the five areas screened*, not a proven billion-pound opportunity or the best possible industry worldwide.

## Five-market screen (qualitative)

| Candidate | Public information | Incumbents | First usable product without permission | Key reason not chosen |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| UK grid-connections | NESO pipeline; network connection dashboards | NESO, National Grid, Gridcog, Olsights | Possible | Technical/regulatory complexity, existing tools, hard to verify decisions |
| Brownfield land | England Planning Data Brownfield Land (37,749 entries on 12 Oct 2026), OGL 3.0 | Searchland, LandTech, Landstack (including free tier), Nimbus | Yes | Crowded and already free for users; no durable unique advantage |
| Mine-waste assets | USGS National Mine Waste Inventory v2, released 1 Oct 2026, CC0 | GeaSpirit, specialist miners/reprocessors | Yes | GeaSpirit already has extensive and near-identical platform; sampling/rights challenges |
| Navigation interference | Public maritime reporting and guidance | Windward, Kpler, Aireon | Partial | High-quality underlying event data often controlled by incumbents |
| Industrial trade-effluent evidence | MOSL DPIDs, Thames consent register, EIR, ICO precedents | MOSL, Waterscan, utilities and environmental consultants | **Yes** | Buyer willingness to pay and data reuse remain unproven |

## Why this entry is differentiated, if anything
This is NOT a national consent database. MOSL already publishes DPID data; Thames Water publishes a public XLSX register; Southern Water publicly offers a register enquiry route. The initial wedge is a usable workflow combining source distinctions, EIR request generation, local tracking and properly caveated evidence-gap reports. The higher-value hypothesis is that environmental diligence buyers will pay for verified site-level intelligence compiled using this workflow. No evidence currently establishes willingness to pay.

## Underlying economic contradiction
Discharge-consent information impacts environmental, commercial and operational decisions, yet verified records can be fragmented across public dashboards, utility records and specific disclosure requests. The potential cost of making a wrong decision may exceed the cost of obtaining and checking data. This second claim needs customer validation.

## Why now
Guardian investigation published 9 October 2026 reported >£340m in annual revenue to water companies from industrial/commercial wastewater. 2024/2025 ICO decisions document enforceable disclosure in two particular cases. This is newsworthy, but newsworthiness is not willingness to pay.

## Competitor findings
- MOSL: national Trade Effluent Discharge Points dashboard, updated monthly; not equivalent to individual consent terms.
- Thames Water: publicly available consent-register spreadsheet and email route for individual consent requests.
- Southern Water: official FAQs list a register access address.
- Waterscan: existing trade-effluent commercial consultancy and data services for industrial clients.
- Environment Agency: public water-discharge permits and different statutory consent route.

## Wedge: who might pay (UNVERIFIED)
Small environmental due-diligence consultancies, property and industrial transaction advisors, insurers and specialist industrial operators making site-specific decisions. Begin with a concrete paid source-verification pilot, not a broad software subscription.

## Business hypothesis and cheapest kill test
H0: At least 10 potential buyers approached will have no economic incentive to buy an independently verified trade-effluent record package.
H1: At least one of 10 qualified potential buyers will explicitly commit to a paid, scoped research pilot within 14 days.

Test: show the working site and a one-page *sourced* sample gap note; ask 10 qualified buyers to identify the most expensive decision they cannot presently make using available records; request a clear paid commission. Record whether they already solve it, their budget, and legal/data concerns. Stop or pivot if none will pay and no compelling unknown has emerged.

## Launch plan, no fictional claims
Day 0: working site and source vault (this package).
Day 1: review against regulator pages and official operator contacts; secure distinct project branding; deploy site.
Day 2: publish an evidence note showing exactly what each official source does and does not contain.
Day 3: publicly announce the live tool *only after deployment and test*; contact qualified people individually without spam or false claims.
Day 4–14: pursue explicit paid pilot, assess data licences and reuse rights before republishing any received material.
